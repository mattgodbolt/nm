# Decisions

Every call the port makes that the original didn't, numbered, with the
reason. The original is the specification; nothing changes quietly.

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026-10-15 | **The screen is MODE 1's pixels on a CRTC narrowed to 256 x 192**, as Chuckie Egg 2's port does: the Spectrum's geometry exactly, 12K at the top of RAM, four colours | The game draws 16 x 16 one-bit blocks on the 8 x 8 grid, and its look is the fine detail of the bricks, the ninjas and the font. MODE 2's eight colours would halve that detail (45% of the graphics' lit pixels pair with an unlit one: docs/brief.md, D1). The colours are chosen per level (to come). |
| 2 | 2026-10-15 | **The 128K version is the specification** | The 128K adds only the AY music (and the driver's own effects entry is never called); everything else is the same code on both machines (docs/research.md). The music is why this project exists. |
| 3 | 2026-10-15 | **The repository is reorganised around the port.** The music conversion's scripts move to `tools/music/` and its data to `music/`; `ninja_music.ssd` stays at the top | Matt asked for it to be a game port first. The music disc stays where it was because the README's jsbeeb link and jsbeeb's own disc list point at that path on `main`. |
| 4 | 2026-10-15 | **The levels are kept as the maps the original's decoder makes, each packed with ZX02**, and the port unpacks a level straight into its map instead of running the original's run-length decoder (&C418) | The original's 24K of run-length bytes don't fit. Packed with ZX02 they would be a little smaller than the maps are, but the port would need a buffer in main RAM for them and the decoder too; the maps unpack straight into the map, which the port needs anyway. `tools/mklevels.py` makes them with a transcription of &C418, and `tools/levelcheck.py` checks that against the original's own decoder for all 50 levels. The pass after it (&C39C: random variants, the start) is transcribed in `level.6502`. |
| 5 | 2026-10-15 | **Colours, for now: each level's palette is black, yellow, its floor colour and cyan**, the Spectrum's inks and papers mapped to those: black to black, yellow to yellow, the floor's ink to the floor colour, any other to cyan. Tile &13's FLASH isn't shown | A first mapping, so the views can be checked: walls are yellow and the floor's colour changes by level (&C357), as in the original; cyan keeps player 1's colour. Everything else (white, green, blue, red, magenta things that aren't the floor) is cyan. To be decided with the screens side by side (docs/brief.md, D1). |
| 6 | 2026-10-15 | **The data the game needs in play goes in a 16K sideways RAM bank** (NDATA, put there by the loader NLOAD, which finds the highest free bank as Chuckie Egg 2's MENU does); the game pages it in for good. With no sideways RAM, for now, the loader says the game needs it | The code, the graphics and the screen fill main RAM; the levels (and later the music) go in the bank. A stock-B edition is for later (docs/brief.md, D2). |
| 7 | 2026-10-15 | **Random numbers come from an 8-bit generator** (shift left, EOR &1D on a carry: `level.6502`'s `random`) **where the original reads R** (LD A,R, at 13 places). For the checks, the original is made to use the same generator (`tools/zx.py`'s `share_random`) | A 6502 has no R, and R depends on the exact instruction count, which the port can't reproduce. With one generator on both, a check can compare everything the random numbers decide (so far, the walls' variants). |
| 8 | 2026-10-15 | **The music is David Whittaker's driver transcribed to 6502** (`music.6502`), **playing the original's song data** (`data/music.6502`, which `tools/mkmusic.py` takes from bank 1), from the VSync interrupt: the driver's code in main RAM, the songs in sideways RAM. Its state is the original's: the voices' &21 bytes at the same offsets, and the shadow AY registers, which are then converted for the SN76489 (decisions 9-12) and written where they changed. The game's own writes to the chip are to hold `mu_busy` (`sound_write`); while it's set the interrupt plays the frame but doesn't write the chip, as in Chuckie Egg 2's decision 46. Not transcribed: the sound effects entry (&C009), which the game never calls (decision 2). For now the game's build plays song 0 from start-up, so it can be heard. A frame of the driver and the chip takes about 2,500 cycles, at most 5,700 [measured 2026-10-15 in the music check's build over song 0's first 1,500 frames, each with the check's own copying of the registers] | Route (b) of docs/brief.md's D3: rule 1 applied to the music. It plays all seven songs, the jingles too, which nm's recorded route couldn't pack, and it is smaller than that route's song 0 alone. `mkmusic.py` finds what the driver reads by playing every song on `tools/music/dw_decode.py`'s Driver: a walk of the bytes alone can't, because the jingles' voices have no ends of their own and run on into the next song's lists, and the song stops on one voice before the others get there. `make music` checks every frame of every song against that Driver, which itself matches the captures in `music/`. |
| 9 | 2026-10-15 | **A tone's period on the SN is the AY's x 1155/1024, rounded** (`mu_period`) | The AY plays 1.7734MHz / (16 x its period), the SN 4MHz / (32 x its period), so the same note is the AY's period x 1.12778 on the SN. 1155/1024 is 1.12793, a quarter of a cent sharp, and needs only adds and shifts. The brief's P + P/8 + P/512 is 1.25 cents flat and, truncated, up to 12 cents flat at the songs' highest notes (periods near 100): the port rounds to the nearest period. An AY period of 0 (which the AY plays as 1) becomes 1. |
| 10 | 2026-10-15 | **The AY's volumes map to the SN's attenuations as ym2sn.py's default does**: each AY step one 2dB step of the SN's, an odd one rounded up (`mu_att`: 15 is 0dB, 14 and 13 2dB down, and so on to 1, 26dB down; 0 silent) | It's what nm's disc sounds like (`tools/music/build.sh` runs `ym2sn.py` without `-a`), which Matt has listened to. The AY's own steps are nearer 3dB at the top, so the instruments' fades are gentler here than on the Spectrum; a table nearer the AY's (its measured levels rounded to the SN's 2dB) would make every volume below 5 the SN's quietest. The table is 16 bytes, easily changed by ear. |
| 11 | 2026-10-15 | **The AY's noise is the SN's white noise, as loud as the loudest voice the mixer gives it to**, at the SN's faster fixed rate for AY noise periods up to 18 and the slower above (`mu_output`); a voice with its tone off is silent on its tone channel. The noise's register is written only when its rate changes and while it's heard, since writing it restarts the SN's shift register | The AY mixes one noise generator into any of its voices; the SN has one noise channel. ym2sn.py does the same but for the level: it gives each AY voice a third of the noise's loudness. That suits music with noise on several voices at once. This music puts it on one voice at a time (the drums, the hi-hat), and on the AY a voice with only noise is as loud as its tone would be, so a third would be 10dB too quiet. The rates' boundary is halfway between the SN's two (7812Hz and 3906Hz): an AY period of 18.9. The drums' alternate frames flip the period's bit 3, so they move between the rates as on the AY they move between pitches. Periodic noise isn't used. |
| 12 | 2026-10-15 | **A tone below the SN's lowest (a period past its ten bits) is played an octave up, or two, until it fits** (`mu_period`), and the octaves are kept in `mu_bass` | The simple route first (docs/brief.md, D3). The SN's lowest note is 4MHz / (32 x 1023), 122Hz. Song 0 has a low tone in 91% of its frames (two at once in 37%, three in 7.5%), one or two octaves below that (notes A#0 to A#2), so this changes the main theme's bass throughout [measured 2026-10-15 on dw_decode.py's frames]. `mu_bass` is the hook for nm's timer bass, which would play those notes at their pitch: a VIA timer for each channel toggling its volume (docs/research/music-driver.md). |
