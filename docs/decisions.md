# Decisions

Every call the port makes that the original didn't, numbered, with the
reason. The original is the specification; nothing changes quietly.

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026-10-15 | **The screen is MODE 1's pixels on a CRTC narrowed to 256 x 192**, as Chuckie Egg 2's port does: the Spectrum's geometry exactly, 12K at the top of RAM, four colours | The game draws 16 x 16 one-bit blocks on the 8 x 8 grid, and its look is the fine detail of the bricks, the ninjas and the font. MODE 2's eight colours would halve that detail (45% of the graphics' lit pixels pair with an unlit one: docs/brief.md, D1). The colours are chosen per level (to come). |
| 2 | 2026-10-15 | **The 128K version is the specification** | The 128K adds only the AY music (and the driver's own effects entry is never called); everything else is the same code on both machines (docs/research.md). The music is why this project exists. |
| 3 | 2026-10-15 | **The repository is reorganised around the port.** The music conversion's scripts move to `tools/music/` and its data to `music/`; `ninja_music.ssd` stays at the top | Matt asked for it to be a game port first. The music disc stays where it was because the README's jsbeeb link and jsbeeb's own disc list point at that path on `main`. |
| 4 | 2026-10-15 | **The levels are kept as the maps the original's decoder makes, each packed with ZX02**, and the port unpacks a level straight into its map instead of running the original's run-length decoder (&C418) | The original's 24K of run-length bytes don't fit. Packed with ZX02 they would be a little smaller than the maps are, but the port would need a buffer in main RAM for them and the decoder too; the maps unpack straight into the map, which the port needs anyway. `tools/mklevels.py` makes them with a transcription of &C418, and `tools/levelcheck.py` checks that against the original's own decoder for all 50 levels. The pass after it (&C39C: random variants, the start) is transcribed in `level.6502`. |
| 5 | 2026-10-15 | **Colours, for now: each level's palette is black, yellow, its floor colour and cyan**, the Spectrum's inks and papers mapped to those: black to black, yellow to yellow, the floor's ink to the floor colour, any other to cyan. Tile &13's FLASH isn't shown | A first mapping, so the views can be checked: walls are yellow and the floor's colour changes by level (&C357), as in the original; cyan keeps player 1's colour. Everything else (white, green, blue, red, magenta things that aren't the floor) is cyan. To be decided with the screens side by side (docs/brief.md, D1). |
| 6 | 2026-10-15 | **The data the game needs in play goes in a 16K sideways RAM bank** (NDATA, put there by the loader NLOAD, which finds the highest free bank as Chuckie Egg 2's MENU does); the game pages it in for good. With no sideways RAM, for now, the loader says the game needs it | The code, the graphics and the screen fill main RAM; the levels (and later the music) go in the bank. A stock-B edition is for later (docs/brief.md, D2). |
| 7 | 2026-10-15 | **Random numbers come from an 8-bit generator** (shift left, EOR &1D on a carry: `level.6502`'s `random`) **where the original reads R** (LD A,R, at 13 places). For the checks, the original is made to use the same generator (`tools/zx.py`'s `share_random`) | A 6502 has no R, and R depends on the exact instruction count, which the port can't reproduce. With one generator on both, a check can compare everything the random numbers decide (so far, the walls' variants). The noises' three (the hit noise's &C8C6 and &C8CC, the burst's &CEF5: a hundred numbers a sound, for its shape only) aren't shared: the port's noise takes its own, so the game's numbers don't depend on the sound. |
| 8 | 2026-10-15 | **The port keeps the original's attributes in memory (`attrs`, as &5800) and draws each cell's pixels in the colours its attribute gives, when they're drawn** | The original's print and some drawing write pixels and leave the attribute as it was, so a cell's colours come from whatever set its attribute last; the port needs to know that. The BBC's pixels take their colours as they're drawn, so where the original changes an attribute after drawing (the title, &C1A6), the port sets it first; anything that recolours what's already drawn has to redraw it. |
| 9 | 2026-10-15 | **The original's busy-wait delays become waits for VSync, for as many frames as the loop took** (the level's pause, `LEVEL_PAUSE`: &C390's two counts of 65,536 round a 34 T-state loop, about 63 frames at the 128K's 70,908 T-states a frame) | A 6502 loop wouldn't take the Z80's time; frames do, whatever the machine. |
| 10 | 2026-10-15 | **The original's variables are kept in its own layout** (`vars_ba`, its &BA29-&BA9F, and `vars_ff`, its &FF2A-&FF61), starting with the tape's own bytes, each address named by its label in disasm/*.ctl (`tools/mkvars.py`) | The original reaches a player's variables by offset from its record (IX), and keeps tables among them (the star, the floor's quarter, the stars' directions). In its layout, the transcription keeps those offsets, and the pass logs can read the same variables on both machines. |
| 11 | 2026-10-15 | **The game takes the OS's workspace below &0D00 for the map and the attributes, and its code starts at &0E00** (NINJA loads at &1900, above the DFS's workspace, and the loader copies it down). A BREAK isn't handled yet | Main RAM below the screen is the game's only room for its code, the graphics and the tables. Once the game owns the interrupts and makes no OS calls, the language's pages and the sound and buffer pages are free, as Chuckie Egg 2 found. |
| 12 | 2026-10-15 | **A pass takes a fixed time, kept by a clock: the System VIA's T1, free-running at the original's median pass** (`PASS_TICK`, irq.6502: 1.22 frames of 70,908 T-states at 3.5469 MHz, measured 2026-10-15 in random play, docs/brief.md D4). The main loop waits for its tick | Nothing in the original waits for the frame: its speed is its code's, padded by the monsters' scan, which burns a fixed delay for every empty tile (36% of play's time). Port the decision (a steady pace), not the padding. Sounds, the slide and the title screens still take their own time inside a pass, as the original's do. |
| 13 | 2026-10-15 | **The controls are a table of BBC keys per player** (magic, up, down, right, left, fire), defaulting to the original's keys where the BBC has them: Q, A, P, O, SPACE and RETURN (the Spectrum's ENTER) for player 1; 9, 8, 7, 6, 0 and SHIFT (CAPS SHIFT) for player 2 | The original rewrites its own code to redefine the keys (each control's port, mask, and CPL or NOP for Kempston): the decision is a key per control per player, which a table keeps. |
| 14 | 2026-10-15 | **The music is David Whittaker's driver transcribed to 6502** (`music.6502`), **playing the original's song data** (`data/music.6502`, which `tools/mkmusic.py` takes from bank 1), from the VSync interrupt: the driver's code in main RAM, the songs in sideways RAM. Its state is the original's: the voices' &21 bytes at the same offsets, and the shadow AY registers, which are then converted for the SN76489 (decisions 15-18) and written where they changed. The game's own writes to the chip are to hold `mu_busy` (`sound_write`); while it's set the interrupt plays the frame but doesn't write the chip, as in Chuckie Egg 2's decision 46. Not transcribed: the sound effects entry (&C009), which the game never calls (decision 2). For now the game's build plays song 0 from start-up, so it can be heard. A frame of the driver and the chip takes about 2,500 cycles, at most 5,700 [measured 2026-10-15 in the music check's build over song 0's first 1,500 frames, each with the check's own copying of the registers] | Route (b) of docs/brief.md's D3: rule 1 applied to the music. It plays all seven songs, the jingles too, which nm's recorded route couldn't pack, and it is smaller than that route's song 0 alone. `mkmusic.py` finds what the driver reads by playing every song on `tools/music/dw_decode.py`'s Driver: a walk of the bytes alone can't, because the jingles' voices have no ends of their own and run on into the next song's lists, and the song stops on one voice before the others get there. `make music` checks every frame of every song against that Driver, which itself matches the captures in `music/`. |
| 15 | 2026-10-15 | **A tone's period on the SN is the AY's x 1155/1024, rounded** (`mu_period`) | The AY plays 1.7734MHz / (16 x its period), the SN 4MHz / (32 x its period), so the same note is the AY's period x 1.12778 on the SN. 1155/1024 is 1.12793, a quarter of a cent sharp, and needs only adds and shifts. The brief's P + P/8 + P/512 is 1.25 cents flat and, truncated, up to 12 cents flat at the songs' highest notes (periods near 100): the port rounds to the nearest period. An AY period of 0 (which the AY plays as 1) becomes 1. |
| 16 | 2026-10-15 | **The AY's volumes map to the SN's attenuations as ym2sn.py's default does**: each AY step one 2dB step of the SN's, an odd one rounded up (`mu_att`: 15 is 0dB, 14 and 13 2dB down, and so on to 1, 26dB down; 0 silent) | It's what nm's disc sounds like (`tools/music/build.sh` runs `ym2sn.py` without `-a`), which Matt has listened to. The AY's own steps are nearer 3dB at the top, so the instruments' fades are gentler here than on the Spectrum; a table nearer the AY's (its measured levels rounded to the SN's 2dB) would make every volume below 5 the SN's quietest. The table is 16 bytes, easily changed by ear. |
| 17 | 2026-10-15 | **The AY's noise is the SN's white noise, as loud as the loudest voice the mixer gives it to**, at the SN's faster fixed rate for AY noise periods up to 18 and the slower above (`mu_output`); a voice with its tone off is silent on its tone channel. The noise's register is written only when its rate changes and while it's heard, since writing it restarts the SN's shift register | The AY mixes one noise generator into any of its voices; the SN has one noise channel. ym2sn.py does the same but for the level: it gives each AY voice a third of the noise's loudness. That suits music with noise on several voices at once. This music puts it on one voice at a time (the drums, the hi-hat), and on the AY a voice with only noise is as loud as its tone would be, so a third would be 10dB too quiet. The rates' boundary is halfway between the SN's two (7812Hz and 3906Hz): an AY period of 18.9. The drums' alternate frames flip the period's bit 3, so they move between the rates as on the AY they move between pitches. Periodic noise isn't used. |
| 18 | 2026-10-15 | **A tone below the SN's lowest (a period past its ten bits) is played an octave up, or two, until it fits** (`mu_period`), and the octaves are kept in `mu_bass` | The simple route first (docs/brief.md, D3). The SN's lowest note is 4MHz / (32 x 1023), 122Hz. Song 0 has a low tone in 91% of its frames (two at once in 37%, three in 7.5%), one or two octaves below that (notes A#0 to A#2), so this changes the main theme's bass throughout [measured 2026-10-15 on dw_decode.py's frames]. `mu_bass` is the hook for nm's timer bass, which would play those notes at their pitch: a VIA timer for each channel toggling its volume (docs/research/music-driver.md). |
| 20 | 2026-10-15 | **Code that isn't in the game's inner loops runs from the sideways RAM bank** (the level's set-up, text, the status, the panel, the colours, the font), **and the tables the drawing uses are made at start-up in OS workspace** | The bank is paged in for good, so code runs there as well as in main RAM, at the same speed; main RAM had filled. |
| 19 | 2026-10-15 | **The beeper's sound effects are played on the SN76489, each as long as the original's, the game waiting as it did**: the throw's chirp and the smart bomb's sweeps as tone channel 1 at each half-cycle's pitch, the step's click as a step of channel 1, the hit noise and the monsters' burst as white noise switched on and off in segments of the original's lengths, from the sound's own random numbers. Each holds `mu_busy`, so the music leaves the chip alone, and the music writes itself again after | The 128K plays its effects on the beeper too (the AY driver's effects entry is never called), so the effects are the original's beeper loops, timed in T-states at the 128K's clock: those become microseconds, timed by the User VIA's T2. A tone at the half-cycle's pitch is the nearest the SN gets to a speaker toggled at that rate. |
| 21 | 2026-10-15 | **The front end, with these differences for now:** the attract screen is whatever is on the screen (the loading screen's legend of items and nasties isn't kept: no room yet), so at power-on the menu comes straight up rather than a blank playfield waiting for a key (Matt booted it and saw nothing to do); the menu's flash swaps two logical colours' physical ones every 8 frames for as long as the original's flashes take (rows 4-23 repainted once so their paper is one logical colour and their ink another), where the original rewrites the attributes twice a frame and shows both at once in bands, and the panel's and the title's colours that share those logical colours flash with them; the Kempston choices say KEMPSTON SELECTED and leave the keys (there's no BBC equivalent yet); EXTEND MODE (CAPS and SYMBOL SHIFT) is CTRL and SHIFT; DELETE deletes in the name (the Spectrum's CAPS SHIFT and 0) | Four colours can't hold the menu's flash, its title and the panel at once; the flash is the menu's point, so it takes two of them. The rest are the BBC's nearest keys, and gaps to fill when there's room and a decision on joysticks. All for Matt to look at. |
| 22 | 2026-10-15 | **Speed: the panel's characters aren't drawn again when they're already shown, and the scroll's block moves copy sixteen bytes at a time through addresses written into the loop.** The screen is the same; the slide is still slower than the original's (a pass with a scroll measured 31.6 frames, the original's 18.6, 2026-10-15) | The original reprints the panel's figures and icons on every touch, up to three times a pass, cheap on the Spectrum and not on the BBC, whose MODE 1 has twice the bytes and colours each: walking took two pass-clock ticks, half the original's speed. The slide moves twice the bytes the Spectrum's does; at about ten cycles a byte it can't match the Spectrum's LDIR. Sliding in fewer, bigger steps would match its length but not its look: for Matt to choose. |
| 23 | 2026-10-15 | **The byte after the map is the original's &C000** (`map_tail`: its code's first byte, &31) | The monsters' scan runs on past the map's last tile with the view at the bottom right, and reads the original's own code as a tile: &31 is a trap door to it, which draws a random number and can spawn. The smart bomb's area can write it. Kept so the game does what the original does there. |
